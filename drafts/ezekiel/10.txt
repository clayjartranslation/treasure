paras: 1 6 9 15 18
head 1: Fire over the City
head 6: The Wheels Again
head 9: The Glory Leaves the Temple
1 I looked at the dome over the cherubim's heads. Above it was something that looked like a throne of deep blue stone.
2 God spoke to the man in linen: "Step between the spinning wheels{a} under the cherubim. Fill both of your hands with burning coals from among the cherubim, and scatter them over the city." I watched him step in.
3 When the man went in, the cherubim were standing on the south side of the temple, and a cloud filled the inner courtyard.
4 Yahweh's glory rose from the cherub and moved to the temple's threshold. The cloud filled the temple, and the bright light of Yahweh's glory filled the courtyard.
5 The sound of the cherubim's wings reached all the way to the outer courtyard. It sounded like El Shaddai{b} speaking.
6 God ordered the man in linen, "Take fire from between the spinning wheels, from among the cherubim." The man entered and took his place beside a wheel.
7 One cherub stretched out a hand to the fire between them, scooped some up, and put it in the linen man's hands. The man took it and went out.
8 Under the cherubim's wings I could see what looked like human hands.
9 Then I saw four wheels beside the cherubim, one next to each cherub. They sparkled like a golden-green gem.
10 All four wheels looked the same, as if one wheel crossed through the middle of another.
11 They could roll in any of the four directions. They did not need to turn. They went in the direction the front of the creature faced, and they never turned as they went.
12 Eyes covered the cherubim's entire bodies, including their backs, hands, and wings. The wheels were full of eyes all the way around, too, all four of them.
13 I heard someone call the wheels "spinning wheels."{c}
14 Each cherub had four faces. One was a cherub's face. The next was a man's. The third was a lion's. The last was an eagle's.{d}
15 The cherubim then lifted up. Here were the very beings I had seen beside the Chebar canal.
16 Whenever a cherub moved, a wheel moved at its side. Whenever one opened its wings to leave the ground, the wheels never left it.
17 If a cherub halted, so did the wheels. If a cherub rose, the wheels rose with it, since the creature's spirit lived in them.
18 Then Yahweh's glory left the temple's threshold and stopped over the cherubim.
19 As I watched, the cherubim opened their wings and left the ground, and the wheels went up with them. At the east gate of Yahweh's house they stopped, and the glory of Israel's God hovered above.
20 These were the creatures I had seen under Israel's God by the Chebar canal. Now I knew they were cherubim.
21 Each had four faces and four wings, with something like human hands under the wings.
22 Their faces matched the faces I had seen by the Chebar canal. Each went straight ahead.
=a The Hebrew word is galgal. It is also in verses 6 and 13.
=b El Shaddai is often translated "God Almighty." The first meaning of Shaddai is uncertain.
=c The Hebrew word galgal suggests something that whirls or rolls.
=d Verse 14 is not found in the Greek. Compare 1:10, where an ox's face takes the place of "the face of a cherub."
