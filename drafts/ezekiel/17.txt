paras: 1 7 11 22
head 1: Two Eagles and a Vine
head 7: The Vine Reaches Out
head 11: The Puzzle Explained
head 22: A New Cedar
1 I received another word from Yahweh.
2 "Mortal one, put a riddle before Israel's family, and tell them a story with a hidden meaning.
3 Tell them, 'The Lord Yahweh says this: / A huge eagle, with wide wings and long flight feathers, / its coat bright with many colors, / flew to Lebanon and seized the crown of a cedar.
4 He snapped off the highest young shoot / and took it to a land where people buy and sell. / There, in a traders' city, he set it in the ground.
5 Next he took some seed from the land itself / and put it in rich earth. / He placed it like a stream-side tree, next to plenty of water.{a}
6 It sprang up and became a vine that spread out, but stayed short. / Its branches bent toward that eagle, / and its roots stayed under him. / So it grew into a vine, put out limbs, and sent up new shoots.
7 "'After that, a second huge eagle arrived, / with wide wings and thick feathers. / Watch: this vine turned its roots toward him. / It stretched its thin stems his way, / leaving the bed where it had been set, / hoping he would water it.
8 Yet that vine already stood in good earth beside plenty of water, / able to grow limbs, grow fruit, / and become a splendid vine.'
9 "Now tell them, 'The Lord Yahweh asks this: Can that vine do well? Will its roots not be yanked free and its fruit torn off, until every fresh leaf withers? You will not need a strong arm or a big crowd to pull it up, roots and all.
10 See, it has been planted a second time. Can it do well? When the wind from the east hits it, will it not wither completely—wither right in the bed where it first grew?'"
11 Yahweh spoke to me once more.
12 "Speak to this stubborn family. Ask them, 'Do you not understand this story?' Then explain: 'The king of Babylon marched into Jerusalem. He seized its king and its leaders and took them home with him to Babylon.{b}
13 From the royal family he picked one man{c} and made a binding promise with him, sealing it with a solemn curse. The strong men of the land he also took away,
14 so the kingdom would stay weak and would not rise up. It would live on only by keeping that promise.
15 But that new king rebelled. Riders went from him to Egypt, asking for horses and a large army. Can he succeed? Can a man who acts this way go free? He broke the promise. Will he go free?
16 "'I swear by My own life, says the Lord Yahweh: Babylon is where he will die—right in the city of the ruler who gave him the crown. That is the ruler whose solemn curse he treated as nothing and whose promise he broke.
17 Pharaoh may bring a huge army and a great force, but he will not help that king in the fight, once dirt ramps are piled up and siege towers are built to take many lives.
18 He treated the oath with contempt when he broke the promise. He had given his hand as a pledge, then did all this anyway. He will not get away.
19 "'So the Lord Yahweh says: I swear by My own life—that solemn curse of Mine that he treated as nothing, and My promise that he broke, I will make him pay for both.
20 My net will drop over him, and My snare will hold him fast. Once he is in Babylon, I will try him there for the way he betrayed Me.
21 His best soldiers{d} in every group will die in battle, and anyone left alive will be scattered on every wind. That is how you will know Yahweh is the One who spoke.'
22 "The Lord Yahweh also says this: / I Myself will break a small branch from the high crown of the cedar and set it in the ground. / From the very top of its young growth I will take a tender shoot / and, with My own hand, set that shoot on a mountain that stands very high.
23 Israel's high mountain is where I will set it. / Limbs will spread from it, fruit will grow on it, / and it will stand as a great cedar. / Winged creatures of every kind will make their home beneath it, / resting in the shadow of its limbs.
24 Then every tree in the field will understand / that I, Yahweh, pull the tall tree down / and lift the short tree up. / A green tree I can wither, / and a withered tree I can make thrive. / I, Yahweh, have spoken, and I will carry it out."
=a The Hebrew word rendered as a stream-side tree is uncertain.
=b This was when Nebuchadnezzar took King Jehoiachin away in 597 BC. See 2 Kings 24:10-17.
=c That man was Zedekiah. See 2 Kings 24:17.
=d Some ancient copies say "best soldiers." The main Hebrew text says "fugitives."
