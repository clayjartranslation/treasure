paras: 1 5 10
head 1: A Lament for Princes
head 5: A Second Young Lion
head 10: The Vine Torn Out
1 "Now you, lift up a funeral song for Israel's princes,
2 and say: / 'Your mother was a lioness among the lions! / She rested among the young males / and raised her cubs there.
3 One cub she raised until he grew strong / and took his place as a young lion. / Tearing prey became what he knew, / and people were what he ate.
4 Word of him reached other nations. / Their pit became his trap. / With hooks they marched him off / into Egypt.{a}
5 "'Once she realized hope was gone, / and that waiting had produced nothing, / she chose another of her cubs / and trained him as a young lion.
6 Among the lions he moved, / a young lion in his own right. / Tearing prey became what he knew, / and people were what he ate.
7 He attacked their strong places{b} / and wrecked their towns. / Everything in the land trembled / when his roar rang out.
8 Around him, nation after nation / rose from their territories. / A net was thrown across his path, / and their pit held him fast.
9 Hooks went into him, and a cage closed around him, / and off he was sent to Babylon's king.{c} / Inside a fortress they held him, / so that his roar would never again be heard / among Israel's hills.
10 "'Your mother was also like a vine in your vineyard,{d} / set in the ground beside the water. / She grew fruit and many branches / because the water was plentiful.
11 Her branches were strong, / strong enough to be a king's staff. / She rose high / above the thick leaves. / Everyone could see how tall she was / and how many limbs she had.
12 But in anger she was pulled up / and thrown on the ground. / The wind from the east dried her fruit. / Her strong limbs snapped off and withered, / and fire burned them up.
13 Now her place is a desert planting, / in ground that is dry and thirsty.
14 Fire has come out from her main stem. / It has burned up her shoots and her fruit. / No strong branch is left, / no staff for a king to hold.' / This song is a lament, and a lament is how it is used."
=a This was King Jehoahaz. Pharaoh Neco took him to Egypt in 609 BC. See 2 Kings 23:31-34.
=b The Hebrew says "He knew his widows." A small change in the letters can mean "he tore down their strong places" or "he harmed their widows."
=c This may be King Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:8-15) or King Zedekiah (2 Kings 25:7).
=d The Hebrew says "in your blood." The wording is unclear. Many readers think it first said "in a vineyard."
