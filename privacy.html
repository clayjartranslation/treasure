Privacy Policy

This page explains in plain language how this free, ad-free Bible site handles information. It is not legal advice. Last updated November 28, 2026.

Terms of Service Account and backup

The short version

You can read and listen with no account. By default, your place, bookmarks, highlights, listening progress, settings, and language choice stay on your device, in this browser. They are not sent to a server we run.

Signing in is optional. The sign-in screen says what will sync. If you sign in, that is your choice to also save those reading items to your account so another device can use them. Text size and light or dark stay on the device. There are no ads, no sale of personal information, and no tracking for advertising. A public visitor total is shown in the footer; that count is described below.

Three other choices are also optional. The daily devotional can be emailed through Brevo, and only after you confirm the address (double opt-in). You can unsubscribe any time. A donation, if you choose to give one, is handled by PayPal. A language other than English opens the page through Google Translate. Nothing is sent to Google Translate unless you pick a language.

The PDF and EPUB downloads need no account and collect no personal data. GitHub Pages, the host, may log technical request data such as IP addresses.

Language translation

The language menu is on every page. English is the language of the site. Choosing English, or never opening the menu, does not load a Google script and does not send the page to Google.

If you pick another language, your browser opens the current page through Google Translate. Google then receives the page address, the page content needed to translate it, your IP address, and ordinary browser data such as the user agent. Google’s privacy policy describes what Google does with that request. Jose Santiago does not receive a copy of that translation request.

The language you pick is stored only in this browser’s local storage, on your device. It is not sent to a server we run, and it is not a cookie we set. Choosing English again, or clearing this site’s data in the browser, removes it. The same choice is remembered on this device so the next visit can open the translated view.

That request is an international transfer. Google may process it in the United States and in other countries. For readers in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the legal basis is your consent, given when you pick the language. You withdraw it by choosing English and clearing the saved choice. Readers in mainland China, Japan, and South Africa can read the matching sections below.

What stays on the device

Until you sign in, and for anything you do not choose to sync, this browser stores:

  • bookmarks and highlights
  • your place in the text
  • listening progress and listening settings, such as speed and voice
  • reading settings such as text size, footnotes, and light or dark
  • the language you picked, if you picked one
  • a short-lived session storage flag so the visitor total is not increased twice in the same visit

That copy lives in the browser’s local storage. Clearing the site’s data in the browser removes it. A backup file, described below, is a way to keep a copy you control. The language choice is not included in the backup file.

Signing in is the opt-in

Nothing in the reading list above is uploaded because you opened a chapter. Sync starts only after you sign in. The sign-in screen states that signing in will also save, to your account:

  • your place in the text
  • bookmarks
  • highlights
  • where you are in the audio, and listening settings such as speed and voice

The account is the same one the Clay Jar Translation can use. Each Bible keeps its own progress. The sign-in session is stored only in this browser (local persistence). It is not placed in a cookie that other websites can read, and it does not by itself sign you in on another device. Another device syncs only after you sign in there too.

If sign-in is not configured for this copy of the site, the sign-in controls are hidden and nothing is uploaded.

Backup file, with no account

From the Account page, and from Bookmarks and highlights → Backup, you can save a JSON file of bookmarks, highlights, your place, listening progress, and settings. You can import that file later. Import merges it with what is already on the device. It does not require an account, and the file is not uploaded by the site. You choose where to keep it. The importer accepts only a Clay Jar Treasure Bible backup and drops entries that are not valid reading data.

Signing out

Signing out asks whether to keep the copy on this device or remove bookmarks, highlights, your place, and listening progress from this device. The account in the cloud is not deleted by signing out. “Delete my data and account” is the separate control that removes the account.

What an account stores

If you sign in, the account is held by Google Firebase. It can include:

  • a user id
  • the email address you used, if you used email or Google
  • a phone number, only if phone sign-in is turned on and you use it (it ships turned off)
  • bookmarks, highlights, reading place, and listening progress and settings, as listed on the sign-in screen

Google sign-in shares the email and name Google provides for that sign-in. Passwords are handled by Firebase Authentication. This site does not keep its own copy of your password.

Phone sign-in is built but switched off. Text-message codes cost money and need Firebase’s paid Blaze plan, so they stay off on the free Spark plan. If the owner later turns phone sign-in on, a code is sent by SMS and Google’s invisible reCAPTCHA checks that a person asked for it. Google’s privacy policy and terms describe reCAPTCHA. Until that switch is on, the site does not ask for a phone number.

Downloads (PDF and EPUB)

Downloading the PDF or EPUB edition from the downloads page does not require an account, and the files do not contain trackers, analytics, or code that sends information back to us. We do not collect personal data when you download or open them. As the host, GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.) may automatically log technical request data, such as your IP address, the date and time, the file requested, and your browser or device type, for security and operation of its service. GitHub handles that data under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We do not receive or use those logs to identify you. Copies of the files hosted on other websites are governed by those sites’ own policies.

Daily devotional by email

You can read the devotional on this site without giving an email address. If you choose “Get it by email,” the form sends your address, and the time zone of the device you used to sign up, to a Brevo signup form. This is double opt-in: Brevo sends a confirmation message first, and you are added only after you tap that message. The signup form stays hidden until this feature is turned on. The form asks you to agree to the Terms of Service and to acknowledge this policy.

Brevo stores the address and the time zone only so the daily devotional can be sent at 4:00 in the morning in that time zone. The time zone is collected to schedule delivery. If no time zone was recorded, 4:00 in the morning, US Eastern time, is used. It has that day’s title, date, verse, reflection, and prayer, and a link back to the devotional page. The address is not sold. It is not used for ads. It is not added to a reading account on this site, and it is not tied to bookmarks or how far you have read. The same applies if you subscribe to the Bible in a Year reading email: Brevo stores that address only to send each day’s readings, after you confirm the link, and you can unsubscribe any time.

Every email has an unsubscribe link, which opens the unsubscribe page. You can use that any time, or use the Unsubscribe control in your inbox. You can also write to contact.clayjar@protonmail.com and ask for the address to be removed.

Sharing verses

The Share button does not load any social-media script, widget, button, pixel, cookie, or tracker. Nothing is sent to a social platform when a page opens or when you open the share menu. The platform buttons are ordinary links that do nothing until you tap one.

  • Device share menu. “Share…” hands the verse text and link to your device’s own share menu (the Web Share API). Your device and the app you pick then handle it under their own terms and privacy policies.
  • Platform buttons. When you tap Facebook, X, Threads, Truth Social, WhatsApp, or Telegram, your browser opens that platform with the verse text and link filled in. From then on, that platform’s own privacy policy applies, and it may receive your IP address, device information, and account details if you are signed in. These links are opened without a referrer, so this site does not tell the platform which page you were reading beyond the link you choose to share.
  • Email and text message. These open your own email or messaging app with the text filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it.
  • Copy. “Copy text and link” puts the text on your device’s clipboard. The Facebook button also copies the text, because Facebook does not accept pre-filled text.
  • Shared links and previews. A shared link points to a small page on this site with the chapter title, a short excerpt, and the logo, so platforms can show a preview, and that page sends the visitor on to the chapter. GitHub Pages, which hosts the site, serves it under GitHub’s privacy statement. The site does not track who opens a shared link.
  • No record. The site does not record what you share, where, or with whom.

Donations

Donations are not tax-deductible. Jose Santiago is an individual, not a 501(c)(3) organization. PayPal collects the payment information it needs under its own privacy policy; we receive only what PayPal shares with a recipient, such as the name, email address, amount, and any note, and use it only to record and acknowledge the gift and to meet legal obligations.

A donation is optional. Reading does not require one. If you choose to give, the payment is handled by PayPal. PayPal receives the payment details, which can include your name, email address, and payment-account data. This site does not receive your full card number. PayPal’s privacy policy describes that processing. A donation is a voluntary gift. It is not a purchase of the Bible, and it is not tied to your reading data.

Who processes data

  • This browser, on your device. The default store for reading data and for the language choice.
  • Google Firebase. Authentication and the account database, only after you sign in. Firebase is a Google service.
  • Google Translate. The page address, page content, IP address, and browser data, only after you pick a language other than English. Google’s privacy policy applies.
  • GitHub Pages. Hosts the website files, including the Bible text. It does not receive your bookmarks or your email address.
  • Hugging Face. Hosts the audio recordings the player may request. A request for a chapter’s audio goes to Hugging Face. Your bookmarks and email address are not sent there. Hugging Face’s privacy policy describes that service.
  • Brevo. Stores a subscriber’s email address and signup time zone and sends the daily devotional, only if you subscribe and confirm. Brevo’s privacy policy describes that service. This site does not add Brevo’s tracking script.
  • PayPal. Processes a donation, only if you choose to give. PayPal’s privacy policy describes that service.
  • Abacus (abacus.jasoncameron.dev). Keeps the public visitor total shown in the footer. The first page of a visit asks it to add one; later pages in that session only read the number. No name or email is sent. The service may see ordinary request data such as an IP address.

There is no advertising network and no analytics product added by this site to follow you across the web. The visitor total is a public count of visits, not a profile of a reader.

Visitor total

The footer can show a public visitor total for this site. On the first page you open in a browser session, the site asks Abacus to add one and return the new total. Later pages in the same session only read the number. No name, email, or cookie is sent for this, and this site does not use the total to identify you or to follow you across the web. Like any ordinary web request, the counter service may see your IP address, browser type, and the time of the request. If the service does not answer, the total is hidden. A flag is kept in this browser’s session storage only so the same visit is not counted twice; it is cleared when the tab or browser session ends.

Children

The site is not directed to children under 13, consistent with the U.S. Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). If you believe a child under 13 has given us personal information, email the contact address and we will delete it. The site is a Bible reader for a general audience. It does not knowingly collect account information, or a devotional email address, from a child under 13. In the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area, and Switzerland, where the age of digital consent is 16, it does not knowingly collect that information from anyone under 16 without a parent or guardian’s consent. Reading without an account does not send account data to us. If you are a parent and believe an account was created for a child, or a child’s email was subscribed, write to the contact address below and ask for it to be deleted.

Lawful basis (GDPR and UK GDPR)

If you only read on the device, this site is not running an account about you. A visitor-total request may still be sent to Abacus so the footer can show the public count; the lawful basis is the legitimate interest in showing that count, using only the ordinary request data the counter service may see. If you sign in, the account data is processed to provide the save-across-devices feature you asked for. That is a contract with you for that feature. You can refuse by not signing in, and you can stop by deleting the account. If you subscribe to the devotional email, the address is processed because you asked for that email and confirmed it. You can withdraw that consent by unsubscribing. If you pick a language, the page is sent to Google Translate because you asked for that translation. You can withdraw that consent by choosing English and clearing the saved language. Where a local law needs a different basis, the basis is the one that law requires for an optional feature you turned on.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask to access, correct, delete, or receive a copy of the account data, and you can object or ask for a restriction. You can also complain to a privacy regulator. Practical ways on this site:

  • A copy you can carry. Save the backup file. That is a portable JSON copy of bookmarks, highlights, place, listening progress, and settings.
  • Erasure. Use Delete my data and account. That removes the sign-in and the saved progress for both Bibles in the account, and the copy of that progress on this device.
  • The devotional email. Use the unsubscribe link in the email, or ask through the contact address.
  • The language choice. Choose English, or clear this site’s data in the browser.
  • Access or a question. Write to contact.clayjar@protonmail.com.

California (CCPA / CPRA)

This site does not sell personal information. It does not share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. There are no ads. If you sign in, the account data is used to keep your place and notes, not to advertise. A devotional subscriber’s email address is not sold. Choosing a language discloses the page and connection data to Google for that translation. That disclosure is not a sale. You may ask for access or deletion using the contact address, the unsubscribe link, and the delete control above.

Brazil (LGPD)

If you are in Brazil, the optional account, the optional email, and the optional translation are processed to provide the feature you requested. You may ask for confirmation, access, correction, and deletion, and you may petition the national authority (ANPD). Use the backup file, Delete my data and account, unsubscribe, or the contact address.

Canada (PIPEDA)

Account data is limited to what the optional sync needs. You can ask for access or a correction through the contact address. You can delete the account yourself. The device copy, including the language choice, remains under your control when you are signed out. A translation request goes to Google only if you pick a language.

Australia (Privacy Act)

If you sign in, account data may be handled by Google Firebase outside Australia, and the public site files are hosted on GitHub Pages. Audio files may be requested from Hugging Face. A translation request may be handled by Google outside Australia, only if you pick a language. A donation may be handled by PayPal. Those companies describe their own handling of overseas storage. You can ask for access or correction through the contact address, and you can delete the account on the Account page.

China (PIPL)

If you are in mainland China, this site does not ask you for an account just to read. Opening a page may send a visitor-total request to Abacus, outside mainland China, so the footer can show the public count. If you pick a language, that choice is your separate consent to send the page address, the page content, your IP address, and browser data to Google, which may process them outside mainland China, for the purpose of showing the translation. You can refuse by leaving the language on English. Nothing is sent to Google Translate if you do not pick a language. The saved language stays in local storage on your device.

Japan (APPI)

If you are in Japan, the purpose of a translation request is to show the language you asked for. Google is the foreign third party that receives the page request, and only after you pick a language. The language preference itself stays on your device and is not provided to Jose Santiago. Opening a page sends a visitor-total request to Abacus, a foreign service, so the footer can show the public count. Sign-in, the devotional email, and a PayPal donation are separate optional features, each described above.

South Africa (POPIA)

If you are in South Africa, Jose Santiago is the responsible party for this site. Google is a third party for a translation request, Brevo is an operator for a confirmed devotional subscription, PayPal is the processor for a donation, Google Firebase is the processor for an account you choose to open, and Abacus keeps the public visitor total. A cross-border transfer to Google happens when you pick a language, because you asked for that translation. A visitor-total request to Abacus is made when you open a page. You may ask for access or correction through the contact address.

Other places

If you live somewhere this page does not name, you keep the rights that place gives you. The contact address is the place to ask for access, correction, or deletion of account data or of a devotional email address. Reading on the device, without sign-in, email, a donation, or a translation, does not send those choices to us. A visitor-total request to Abacus may still be made when you open a page.

International transfers

Firebase, GitHub Pages, Hugging Face, Brevo, Google Translate, PayPal, and Abacus may process data in the United States and in other countries. Their own terms describe how they handle those transfers. This site does not run a separate server for bookmarks. If you do not sign in, reading data is not transferred to those account systems. An email address is sent to Brevo only if you subscribe. A page is sent to Google Translate only if you pick a language. A payment is sent to PayPal only if you donate. A visitor-total request is sent to Abacus when you open a page.

How long data is kept

The copy on the device stays until you clear it, sign out and choose to remove it, or delete the account (which also clears that progress on this device). The language choice stays until you change it or clear the site’s data. The visitor-total flag stays only until the browser session ends. The public total is kept by Abacus. Account data in Firebase stays until you delete the account. A devotional email address stays at Brevo until you unsubscribe or ask for it to be deleted. PayPal keeps donation records under PayPal’s own schedule. The Bible text and audio files stay published as part of the site. This site does not keep its own server logs of what you read.
